# Find the White Rabbit — Glossary

## Exhibition

The single online digital photography exhibition, rather than a general-purpose portfolio or image library.

## Artwork

One publicly approved photograph in the curated exhibition sequence. It has a stable media key and its visitor-facing descriptive metadata.

## Curated sequence

The intentional emotional ordering of the exhibition's 14 MVP artworks. It is not a chronological feed. The final artwork is a visible concluding work before the ending message.

## Final artwork

The visible concluding artwork. It has no White Rabbit. The visitor explicitly chooses to leave it for the ending message and restart control.

## White Rabbit

The exhibition's primary narrative invitation to move forward. In ordinary motion settings, it is the visual next-work cue; it must never obscure protected viewing or navigation areas.

## White Rabbit mode

The ordinary-motion exhibition mode. The visitor advances only by following the visible White Rabbit; it has no persistent next-work control and no direct previous/next keyboard navigation.

## Direct navigation

An intentional visitor-controlled route to an artwork, including the Dock, Home, and the reduced-motion next-work control. It does not replace the White Rabbit as the ordinary visual cue.

## Dock

The visitor-opened horizontal artwork rail, entered from the bottom-centred trigger. It provides direct navigation without becoming a conventional gallery grid.

## Paused rabbit session

The remaining viewing or waiting time for the White Rabbit while the Dock or About layer is open. It resumes when the layer closes. A rabbit that was already visible is dismissed rather than preserved behind a layer.

## Reduced-motion mode

The visitor preference in which non-essential motion, including the White Rabbit's random reveal, is removed and an explicit next-work control remains available.

## Exhibition media

Public, web-ready JPEG derivatives delivered to visitors for the first release. They are distinct from the private source originals, which are never published in the application repository or public storage.

## Artwork ownership

The 14 MVP artworks are original photographs owned by the exhibition creator.

## Public artwork metadata

The title, sequence label, bilingual exhibition text, and accurate alt text shown to visitors. Photographer name, location, year, and equipment information are excluded from the first release.

## Bilingual presentation

Short Chinese and English exhibition text shown together in clearly separated sections on the same page. It does not require language-specific routes or a language switcher in the first release.

## Content risk review

The manual, artwork-by-artwork review before public release for identifiable, sensitive, or otherwise concerning people depicted in a photograph. A concerning artwork is excluded from the launch sequence or made non-identifying before release.

## Link-only preview

The pre-release exhibition available to people who possess its URL but not intentionally listed or promoted publicly. It is used for visual and content approval before the formal domain is connected.
